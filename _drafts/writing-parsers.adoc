I am currently quite busy with architecture mapping in my day job and while
drawing and mapping is fun, I am sometimes a bit afraid of getting
rustyfootnote:[Pun absolutely intended..].
To fight this, I occasionally take a stroll through my projects and exhume
some older ones in my spare time.

Among my parked project rots something I've creatively dubbed {spotql}[spotql],
a highly ambitious project to combine a {spotify}[Spotify] client and {psql}[psql]
via {wireprot}[Postgres' wire protocol].

TIP: I am well aware of the long list of really good libraries, like
{pgwire}[pgwire], but  I ask you where is the fun, if I cannot combine two
completely unrelated things and invent the wheel again?

This whole project naturally involves writing a parser, hence the name of
the blogpost you are currently reading.

So over the course of the next few paragraphs I am going to delve a bit in how
to write parsers with {rust}[Rust], starting from the most naive version, over
to something you probably could learn at the university to more or less
brainfuckfootnote:[The feeling, not the language, mind you!].

protocols and {endianess}[endianess], but to keep the focus and to get
side-tracked too much, let us just pick the simplest line:

[source,sql]
----
SELECT * from spotify;
----
